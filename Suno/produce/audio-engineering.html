PHASE 6 / PRODUCTION + AUDIO ENGINEERING

AUDIO ENGINEERING

Diagnose and improve the generated production as an engineering problem: balance, dynamics, space, artifacts, vocal clarity, stereo behavior and repair workflow.

AUDIO FIX V6PRODUCE GUIDEFIX / TEST

01 — THE V6 AUDIO REALITY

Suno's official V6 announcement positions v6 as its flagship model and says it is designed for reliable, precise and polished output. The same release also adds stronger editing and reference workflows. At the same time, early community reports are mixed: some users report cleaner, wider mixes while others report muffled vocals, excessive compression, hiss, midrange buildup, reverb and late-song degradation.

Engineering rule: never assume a production problem is caused by prompting. First classify the artifact, then choose the smallest repair workflow.

02 — MIX DIAGNOSTIC ORDER

  1. Lead vocal intelligibility
  2. Low-end buildup / mud
  3. Midrange congestion
  4. High-frequency harshness / hiss
  5. Dynamics / over-compression
  6. Reverb / depth
  7. Stereo width / phase
  8. Clipping / distortion / digital artifacts
  9. Arrangement density
  10. Overall loudness and balance

Fixing the wrong layer wastes generations. A buried vocal may be an arrangement-density problem rather than a “vocal prompt” problem.

03 — LOW-END + MUD

Listen for kick, bass, low synths, low guitar, room energy and stacked low-mid instruments competing for the same space.

controlled low end, defined kick and bass separation, clean low mids, no excessive low-mid buildup, instruments remain individually intelligible

This is a descriptive target, not a substitute for actual post-production EQ.

04 — MIDRANGE CONGESTION

Congestion often presents as a “boxy,” crowded or fatiguing mix. Identify whether the conflict is lead vocal + guitars, vocal + synths, stacked harmonies, or dense drums.

clear midrange separation, restrained competing layers, present lead vocal, instruments remain distinct without harsh masking

05 — HIGH-END / HISS / HARSHNESS

Do not equate “bright” with “good.” Test whether clarity comes from useful presence or from added hiss and brittle upper-mid energy.

clear controlled high frequencies, smooth presence, crisp transients without brittle harshness, no audible hiss

Community reports during the V6 rollout include both hiss complaints and users who report no such issue, so classify this as observed variability rather than a universal model property.

06 — DYNAMICS + COMPRESSION

Listen for whether the track becomes progressively louder, flatter or more crowded as it develops.

controlled dynamics, preserved transient impact, natural loudness progression, avoid excessive compression or pumping

When a generation becomes over-compressed, compare another generation before assuming a single prompt phrase can repair it.

07 — VOCAL PRESENCE

Use a three-way diagnosis:

  • Vocal itself: timbre, articulation, distortion, pitch and texture.
  • Arrangement: instruments occupying the same range.
  • Mix: level, dynamics, ambience and masking.
lead vocal forward and intelligible, centered, stable in level, clear consonants, instruments leave space around the vocal

08 — REVERB + DEPTH

Too much ambience can make a clear vocal sound distant. Too little can make a production feel unnaturally dry.

controlled studio ambience, short-to-medium depth, lead vocal remains close and intelligible, no washy tail covering the mix

09 — STEREO WIDTH

Describe width by role instead of demanding “wide everything.”

centered kick, bass and lead vocal; supporting synths and guitars moderately wide; backing vocals wider than lead; stable center image

10 — MONO / PHASE THINKING

A very wide result can collapse when summed. Test important elements in mono when possible. Look for disappearing guitars, thinning vocals, unstable bass and drastic level changes.

This is an engineering diagnostic, not a claim that Suno exposes direct phase controls in generation.

11 — TRANSIENTS

Kick, snare, plucks and consonants need attack to read clearly. If everything is smeared, diagnose transient loss separately from overall loudness.

defined transients, controlled attack, punchy drums, articulate consonants, no excessive smear

12 — ARTIFACT TAXONOMY

  • hiss / broadband noise
  • warbling or shimmer
  • stuttering
  • clicks / ticks
  • clipping
  • phasey or hollow tone
  • vocal doubling artifacts
  • instrument disappearance
  • unexpected instrument substitution
  • late-song degradation
  • abrupt loudness change
  • over-reverberation

Log the exact timestamp. “Bad audio” is too broad to be actionable.

13 — TIMESTAMPED DIAGNOSTIC

00:00–00:20  baseline
00:20–00:45  first density change
00:45–01:10  vocal quality
01:10–01:35  chorus impact
01:35–02:00  mid-song stability
02:00–END     late-song behavior

For every failure record: when it starts, what changes, whether it persists, whether another generation reproduces it, and whether stems isolate the source.

14 — REFERENCE AUDIO

V6 supports audio/reference-driven workflows. Use references to communicate feel, arrangement ideas or source material, but separate reference influence from generation prompt when testing causality.

CONTROL: prompt only
TEST A: same prompt + reference audio
TEST B: same prompt + different reference
TEST C: reference + minimal prompt

15 — EDIT / REPLACE / REMASTER DECISION TREE

ProblemFirst workflow to testWhy
One lyric/word is wrongSingle lyric updateSmallest intervention
One section is wrongEdit / Replace sectionPreserve what already works
Whole track structure works but sound needs refinementRemasterOfficially designed for subtle sonic variations
Need isolated instrument/vocalStems / isolate workflowDiagnose and continue in DAW
Fundamental generation is wrongRegenerateRepairing a bad foundation can cost more than restarting

16 — REMASTER AS A PRODUCTION TOOL

Suno's current Remaster documentation says Remaster is intended for subtle variations when the structure, lyrics and performance are already acceptable, including improving overall sound quality, mix/balance, sonic texture, clarity and pronunciation.

Use it when the song is right but the presentation is wrong. Do not use it as an excuse to regenerate a fundamentally broken arrangement.

17 — STEM-BASED RESCUE

When available, stems let you determine whether the defect belongs to the vocal, drums, bass, music or another layer. A stem can also reveal that what sounds like a “mastering” issue is actually baked into one source layer.

FULL MIX PROBLEM
→ inspect stems
→ isolate suspect layer
→ identify source of artifact
→ repair in Studio/DAW if practical
→ reassemble
→ compare against original full mix

18 — ARRANGEMENT DENSITY VS MIX QUALITY

More instruments can create apparent degradation without the underlying generation becoming technically worse. A dense climax may mask the vocal and compress perceived space.

CONTROL: full arrangement
TEST: same musical idea with fewer simultaneous layers
COMPARE: clarity, vocal intelligibility, stereo separation, low-mid buildup

This distinction matters because “make the mix clearer” may be less effective than reducing competing arrangement density.

19 — LOUDNESS IS NOT QUALITY

A louder generation can initially sound “better” in a quick comparison. Level-match before deciding which result is cleaner. Evaluate transient preservation, distortion, vocal clarity, low-end definition and fatigue.

20 — POST-PRODUCTION BOUNDARY

Generation prompting can communicate a sonic target. It cannot replace a DAW when you need exact EQ frequencies, compression ratios, automation curves, surgical restoration, precise stereo control or guaranteed mastering specifications.

Important: this guide does not modify or replace the dedicated Audio Fix V6 module. That module remains its own locked workflow.

22 — PHASE 6 ENGINEERING RULE

Classify → timestamp → isolate → repair the smallest layer → compare level-matched results → replicate. Prompting is one tool in the production chain, not a magic mastering button.

Audio & References

Use audio, images, video, songs and playlists as creative inputs when the idea is easier to demonstrate than describe — and use controlled testing when V6 audio quality goes sideways.

Reference thinking

References are starting information, not a promise of literal copying. Give detailed guidance about what should be preserved or transformed. When multiple references are used, assign each a role instead of expecting perfect prioritization.

Reference A → vocal character Reference B → drum groove Reference C → synth texture Reference D → emotional atmosphere

Upload strategy

Use a clean, representative source. Trim irrelevant silence, avoid unnecessary clipping, identify the important section, and decide whether the goal is preserving rhythm, melody, timbre, performance, or overall vibe.

Exact rescue prompt

recreate the original audio exactly as sung and performed, identical vocal timbre phrasing dynamics intensity and emotion, identical instrument playing style technique feel and arrangement, identical overall musical energy and mood, ultra-professional studio mastering, lossless hi-fi, reference-grade mix, 24-bit digital clean, dynamic range 12dB+, wide cinematic stereo imaging, crystal-clear highs, high quality bass, perfectly balanced mids, zero mud, zero muddiness, zero low-end buildup, zero artifacts, zero compression artifacts, zero digital hiss, zero clipping, no frequency pollution, no resonance, no phase issues, fully transparent final master, radio broadcast quality, concert hall clarity, warm analog depth + razor-sharp digital precision, present lead vocals, wide and airy soundstage, high-fidelity studio production, crisp transients, layered instruments with clear separation, stable tonal balance and loudness

What this test is actually diagnosing

  • bad or degraded source audio
  • muddy output / low-mid buildup
  • distortion and clipping
  • harshness and high-frequency artifacts
  • weak vocal fidelity or changed vocal timbre
  • loss of original phrasing, dynamics or performance feel
  • unwanted reinterpretation of the source
  • reference-audio degradation during generation
  • unexpected V6-MINI/Sample behavior

Preserve the performance vs. preserve the idea

A result can preserve the song's musical idea while changing the actual performance. When evaluating this recipe, separately score vocal timbre, phrasing, dynamics, intensity, instrument technique, arrangement, transients, tonal balance and artifacts.

Controlled A/B test

BASELINE ↓ V6-MINI + Sample + Whole Track ↓ Weirdness 0% / Style 86% / Audio 86% ↓ OTHER OPTIONS OFF ↓ Generate multiple trials ↓ Compare against source ↓ Change ONE variable ↓ Repeat

If it fails

ONE LYRIC → Single Lyric Edit ONE SECTION → Replace Section ENDING → Crop / Extend ONE INSTRUMENT → Stems / Studio BAKED REVERB / DELAY → Get Dry MIX PROBLEM → Studio EQ / Compression / Effects / Automation OVERALL POLISH → Remaster STILL BAD → Abandon the generation rather than endlessly regenerating the same failure
Important: descriptive mastering language in the prompt does not guarantee literal 24-bit output, an exact 12 dB dynamic range, exact LUFS, exact phase behavior or any other engineering measurement. Judge the rendered audio itself. Prompt for audible characteristics; measure exact engineering targets in Studio or a DAW.

Source quality vs. generation quality

A clean source can still produce a poor Mini V6 generation. If the source is clean but the output has mud, harshness, clipping, phase instability, smeared transients or other defects, record it as a generation/audio-quality failure rather than automatically blaming the source. Conversely, prompt wording cannot recreate information that was never present in a damaged input.
